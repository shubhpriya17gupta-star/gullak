Methodology

Simple maths you can check.

Every figure in Gullak comes from one of the formulas below, applied only to the numbers you enter. There are no hidden inputs, no predictions, and no stock or fund recommendations. Inside the Planner, each result also has a “How was this calculated?” panel that shows the same working with your own numbers, so you never need to come here to get an answer.

Your budget

Two plain descriptions of your monthly position. Neither is a target.

F1Planner · budget

Monthly surplus

surplus = income − essentials − EMIs

What is left each month after essential spending and loan repayments. A negative result is shown as a deficit, with no further judgement.

Worked example
₹80,000 − ₹45,000 − ₹15,000 = ₹20,000 a month
F2Planner · budget

Emergency cover

cover = emergency savings ÷ (essentials + EMIs)

How many months your emergency savings would pay for essentials and EMIs. A description, not a target. If you enter no essentials or EMIs, it shows “n/a”.

Worked example
₹2,40,000 ÷ ₹60,000 = 4.00 months

Growth

The rates are always your own assumptions, applied once a year. They are illustrations, not forecasts.

F4Planner · results

Future value

value = amount × (1 + rate) ^ years

One amount growing at an assumed yearly rate, with each year’s gain left invested. The year-by-year table (F5) applies the same formula to each year, and its last row equals this value. If you enter the horizon in months or days, the whole years compound as above and the leftover part of a year earns the rate as simple interest, e.g. 18 months = 1 whole year, then 6/12 of a year (a year is taken as 365 days).

Worked example
₹1,00,000 × 1.10 ^ 10 = ₹2,59,374
18 months: ₹1,00,000 × 1.10 × (1 + 0.10 × 6/12) = ₹1,15,500
F6Planner · results

Gain, and growth on growth

gain = value − amount
simple = amount × (1 + rate × years)
growth on growth = value − simple

The part of the gain that comes from earning returns on earlier returns, compared with the same rate added as simple interest.

Worked example
Gain ₹2,59,374 − ₹1,00,000 = ₹1,59,374
Simple ₹1,00,000 × (1 + 0.10 × 10) = ₹2,00,000
Growth on growth ₹2,59,374 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹59,374
F7Consistency check

Compound annual growth rate

CAGR = (end value ÷ start value) ^ (1 ÷ years) − 1

The single steady yearly rate that links a start and an end value. It is used to confirm that a projection really grows at the rate you assumed.

Worked example
(₹2,59,374 ÷ ₹1,00,000) ^ (1 ÷ 10) − 1 = 10.00% a year
F8Planner · results

Real rate and real value

real rate = (1 + rate) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1
real value = value ÷ (1 + inflation) ^ years

Growth after rising prices are taken out, so you see what the money would buy, not just its rupee value. The exact form is used, not the shortcut “rate − inflation”.

Worked example
1.10 ÷ 1.04 − 1 = 5.77% a year
₹2,59,374 ÷ 1.04 ^ 10 = ₹1,75,224 in today’s money
F9Planner · risk

Return a goal would need

required rate = (goal ÷ amount) ^ (1 ÷ years) − 1

The steady yearly rate needed to reach a goal you set. It is the reverse of F4 and feeds the risk-requirement score. It does not say any investment can deliver that rate.

Worked example
Goal ₹2,00,000 from ₹1,00,000 in 10 years: (2) ^ (1 ÷ 10) − 1 = 7.18% a year

Falls and recovery

What a fall does to a value, and what it takes to get back. The falls are hypothetical sizes you choose, not past events.

F10Planner · results

Hypothetical fall

after fall = value × (1 − fall)

What a value would be straight after a fall of the size you choose. Shown for today’s amount and for the end of the horizon.

Worked example
₹2,59,374 × (1 − 25%) = ₹1,94,531
F11Break-even calculator

Gain needed to recover

gain needed = fall ÷ (100% − fall)

A fall is measured on the bigger starting amount, but the recovery is measured on the smaller amount left, so the gain needed is always larger than the fall.

Worked example
25% ÷ (100% − 25%) = 33.33%

F12Break-even calculator

Years to recover at an assumed rate

smallest whole k where (1 + rate) ^ k ≥ start ÷ now

Assumes returns resume at a steady rate after the fall. It is arithmetic only, not a statement about how markets behave, and recovery is never promised. If the rate is 0% or less, it never recovers at that rate.

Worked example
After a 25% fall, start ÷ now = 1.3333. At 10%: 1.10 ^ 3 = 1.331 (just short), 1.10 ^ 4 = 1.464 (enough) → 4 years (about 3.02 on a decimal estimate)

Trade costs

One buy and one sell of listed shares held as delivery on NSE, worked the way a contract note shows it.

F13Charges calculator

Cost of one stock trade

you pay = buy value + buy-side charges
you receive = sell value − sell-side charges
you keep = (you receive − you pay) − tax − cess

Charges include brokerage, exchange charges, the SEBI fee, GST, STT and stamp duty, each with its own rate. Tax applies only if the taxable gain is above zero. The rates are labelled with the date they were checked, and the full list is on the Charges page.

Worked example
Enter your own buy and sell prices on the Charges page to see every line, to the paisa.

Returns

F14Planner · results

Investment return

return = [(current value − investment) ÷ investment] × 100

The gain or loss as a share of what was invested, over the whole period. It is not a yearly rate. For a yearly rate over several years, use the compound annual growth rate (F7).

Worked example
(₹2,59,374 − ₹1,00,000) ÷ ₹1,00,000 × 100 = 159.37%

How the numbers are handled, and what is not included

  • Money is calculated exactly in whole paise and rounded (half-up) only for display.
  • Rates are your own assumptions, applied once a year. Gullak does not forecast returns.
  • Costs, charges and taxes are not included in the growth figures. The Charges calculator is the one place that covers them.
  • The Charges calculator does not include depository charges, BSE trades, intraday trades, surcharge, the annual long-term gains exemption, or loss set-off.
  • No prices, index history or ticker are shown, because exchange and index data need a licence we do not hold.
