Why the climb is longer

The gain is measured on what is left.

Say ₹100 falls 25% and becomes ₹75. A 25% gain on ₹75 is only ₹18.75, which gets you to ₹93.75. To get back to ₹100 you need ₹25 on top of ₹75, and ₹25 is one third of ₹75, a 33.33% gain.

The deeper the fall, the more lopsided it gets. That is why the climb in the valley grows longer than the fall.

Gain needed = fall ÷ (100% − fall)
25% ÷ 75% = 33.33%
How was this calculated?
Formula F11 in the project methodology. With amounts, the gain is (start − now) ÷ now, worked from your amounts directly. Calculations use exact whole-number arithmetic on paise and basis points, and results are rounded half-up to 2 decimals for display only. The optional recovery time (F12) finds the first whole year in which (1 + rate)^years reaches start ÷ now, and also shows a decimal estimate. Not included: costs, charges, taxes.
