Before you start

To buy shares in India you need two accounts, usually opened together with one broker:

Trading account
Where you place buy and sell orders. It connects you to the stock exchanges (NSE, BSE) through your broker.
Demat account
Where your shares are held in electronic form, at a depository (NSDL or CDSL), through a depository participant such as your broker.

What a broker will ask for

  • PAN card
  • Aadhaar linked to your mobile number, for online (e-KYC) verification
  • Bank account details: the account number and IFSC code, checked online with a ₹1 deposit
  • A mobile number and email address you use
  • Your signature and a photo, captured during the process
Check the broker first. Only open an account with a stockbroker registered with SEBI. You can look up any broker's registration on the SEBI website before you share documents.

Words you just met

NSE and BSE
India's two main stock exchanges, the National Stock Exchange and BSE (formerly the Bombay Stock Exchange). Many companies are listed on both. Your broker sends your order to the exchange you pick.
Delivery and intraday
Delivery means you take the shares into your demat account and can hold them. Intraday means you open and close the trade on the same day, so no shares are delivered. You can also sell first and buy back later the same day, which is called short selling.
Square-off
Closing an intraday position with the opposite trade. If you do not, the broker does it for you shortly before the market closes, at whatever the price is then.
Trade-for-trade segment
Shares in this segment (the "BE" series on NSE) must be settled by delivery for every trade, so intraday is not allowed. Newly listed shares from IPOs of up to ₹250 crore trade here for their first 10 days. Coral Solar on this desk is set up like that.
Market order
Buy or sell straight away at the best price available. Quick, but the price you get can differ from the one you saw.
Limit order
Buy only at your price or lower, or sell only at your price or higher. It may never be executed. Here, an order that is not executed expires when the market closes, just like a day order.
Blocked money
Money set aside for an open buy order, so you cannot spend it twice. It comes back if the order is cancelled or expires.
T+1 settlement
The standard rule in India: shares you buy are credited to your demat account on the next trading day, and money from a sale is paid out on the next trading day. Broker apps often show the shares in your holdings straight away, marked as not yet settled, and may let you use sale money for new buys the same day. You can withdraw it only after settlement.
T+0 settlement
An optional same-day settlement that SEBI allows for a list of the largest companies' shares, if your broker offers it. Shares and money then settle on the trade day itself. This desk uses T+1 only.
Contract note
The bill for your trades: price, quantity, brokerage, taxes and charges. Your broker must send it within 24 hours of the trade, usually by email on the evening of the trading day.

How this desk is simpler than real life

  • Every executed order costs ₹20 brokerage plus 18% GST, so ₹23.60, the figures from our trade cost calculator. Real brokers charge differently, and exchange fees, securities transaction tax, stamp duty and depository charges are not included.
  • Capital-gains tax is not shown. For resident investors the broker does not deduct it; you pay it when you file your income-tax return. The trade cost calculator shows it for one trade.
  • Money from selling demat shares can be spent here only from the next trading day. Many brokers let you use it for new buys straight away.
  • Orders are executed in full at the price on screen. Real orders meet other buyers and sellers and can be executed in parts.
  • Intraday here needs the full value of the trade. Real brokers often ask for only part of it (margin) and decide which shares allow intraday. A SEBI study found that about 7 in 10 individuals who traded intraday in shares lost money in 2022–23. Intraday profits are also taxed differently from delivery gains: as speculative business income, not capital gains.
  • The real market is open 9:15 am to 3:30 pm on trading days. Here, a day lasts until you press "Close market".
